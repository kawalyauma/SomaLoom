## Surat al-Fil: Meaning and the Story of the Elephant

### What you will learn
- Retell the main events described in Surat al-Fil (Qur'an 105).
- Explain the meaning of key words in the story.
- Distinguish what the Qur'an says from details told in historical accounts.
- Explain why pride and harmful actions should be avoided.

### The story in simple words
Surat al-Fil speaks about **the people of the elephant**. Traditional accounts describe an army marching against Makkah, where the Ka'bah stands. The Qur'an asks people to think about what happened to their plan. Allah sent birds against the army. The birds struck them with stones of baked clay. The army was defeated and became like eaten-up straw.

In traditional accounts of this event, the leader is called **Abraha**. His name is **not stated in the five verses**. Keep that difference clear when telling the story.

### Follow the events
**An army approaches Makkah**; then **its plan fails**; then **birds are sent against it**; finally **the army is defeated**.

### Words to know
- **Invade:** enter a place by force in order to take control.
- **Mischief:** actions that cause harm or trouble.
- **Baked clay:** clay hardened by heat.
- **Defeat:** to overcome an attacking force.
- **Pride:** thinking so highly of yourself that you look down on others.

### What the surah teaches
1. Power and large numbers do not make a harmful plan right.
2. Allah's power is greater than human strength.
3. People should not attack or destroy places of worship.
4. We should avoid pride and use our abilities to help others.

### Look carefully at the sequence
Use the arrows to retell the story without changing the order. The arrows show the sequence of events, not a drawing of any sacred person.
