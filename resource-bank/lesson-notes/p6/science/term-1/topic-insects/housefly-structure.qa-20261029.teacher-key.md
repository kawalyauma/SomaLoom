# Teacher key — The structure of a housefly

## Teaching checks
An adult housefly is a dipteran insect. Its thorax bears **one pair of functional wings**; the second wing pair is modified into **halteres**. It has six jointed legs. It breathes through spiracles and tracheal tubes. The diagram is simplified and should not be used to count every abdominal segment.

## Practice answers
1. **Head, thorax, abdomen.**
2. **Three pairs; six legs.**
3. **Halteres**, the small modified hind wings.
4. Air enters **spiracles** and travels through branching **tracheal tubes** to body tissues.
5. Housefly: six legs and three main body regions. Spider: eight legs and two main body regions.
6. Accept labels placed on the corresponding structures: head at front, thorax in middle, abdomen behind, wing attached to thorax, leg attached to thorax, compound eye on head.
7. Any two: cover food, dispose of rubbish in lidded bins, keep latrines clean, wash hands, clean food preparation areas.
8. **An adult housefly has one pair of functional wings; the hind wings are modified into halteres.**

## Diagram validation
The original side-view diagram labels eye and antenna on head, legs/wing/haltere on thorax, spiracles on abdomen. Count six legs in the drawing. Do not claim every housefly feature is to scale.
