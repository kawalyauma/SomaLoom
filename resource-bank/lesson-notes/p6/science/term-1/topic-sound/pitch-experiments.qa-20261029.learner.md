# Primary Six Science — Investigating pitch and loudness

## Sound begins with vibration
A **vibration** is a quick movement backwards and forwards. A plucked ruler, stretched elastic band or drum skin vibrates and produces sound.

**Pitch** tells whether a sound is high or low. It depends mainly on **frequency**: faster vibrations produce a higher pitch. **Loudness** tells how loud or soft a sound seems. It depends mainly on **amplitude**, the size of the vibration. Frequency and amplitude are different.

## Investigation A: The length of a vibrating ruler
**You need:** a flexible plastic ruler and a strong desk. Work with a teacher. Do not strike or bend the ruler near another pupil's face.

1. Put most of the ruler on the desk, leaving a short part hanging over the edge.
2. Hold the part on the desk firmly. Gently pluck the free end.
3. Listen to the pitch, not how loud it is.
4. Increase the length hanging over the edge. Pluck it with a similar small movement.
5. Compare the sounds. The shorter free end usually vibrates faster and gives a higher pitch; the longer free end gives a lower pitch.

**Keep the same:** ruler, desk, and roughly the strength of each pluck. **Change:** length of the free end. **Observe:** pitch.

## Investigation B: The strength of a vibration
1. Use the same ruler and the same free length.
2. Pluck it gently. Listen.
3. Pluck it a little more firmly without bending it dangerously.
4. The larger vibration usually gives a louder sound. The pitch should remain approximately the same.

**Keep the same:** ruler and free length. **Change:** size of the pluck. **Observe:** loudness.

## Remember
- Shorter vibrating parts often give higher pitch under similar conditions.
- Bigger vibrations usually give louder sound.
- Sound needs matter to travel through. It cannot travel through a vacuum.
- Protect your ears: never conduct an experiment with painfully loud sounds.
