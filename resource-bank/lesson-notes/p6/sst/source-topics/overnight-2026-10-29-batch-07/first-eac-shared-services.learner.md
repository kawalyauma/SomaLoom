## What was the first EAC?
The East African Community (EAC) was formed in 1967 by Uganda, Kenya and Tanzania. The first EAC ended in 1977. The three countries wanted to work together and share important services.

A **shared service** is a service organised for the benefit of more than one country. Sharing could reduce costs and help people travel, trade and communicate across borders.

## Services that East Africans shared
**Railways and harbours:** Trains carried people and goods. Harbours helped ships load and unload goods. These links were especially useful to landlocked Uganda, which uses routes through neighbouring countries to reach the sea.

**Posts and telecommunications:** Postal services carried letters and parcels. Telephone and other communication links helped people and businesses exchange messages.

**Air transport:** East African Airways connected towns and cities by air, helping travel, trade and communication.

**Customs and revenue:** Cooperation on customs helped organise the collection of duties on goods entering the region. A customs duty is a tax charged on certain imported goods.

**Research and education:** The partner countries also cooperated in research, examinations, publications and other specialist work.

## Why were shared services useful?
1. A trader could use transport links to send goods to customers in another country.
2. Families and offices could send letters or messages across borders.
3. Countries could share specialist skills and the costs of some services.
4. People could travel more easily for work, learning and family visits.

## Do not confuse the two periods
The **first EAC (1967–1977)** had shared organisations such as East African Railways and Harbours and East African Airways. The **revived EAC (from 2000)** is a different period of cooperation. Its present institutions and programmes are not identical to those of 1967–1977.

## Worked examples
**Example 1:** A Ugandan trader sends produce by rail towards a seaport. Which shared service helped? **Railways and harbours**, because trains and ports move goods.

**Example 2:** A family sends a letter from Kampala to Nairobi. Which service helped? **Posts**, because postal services carry letters.
