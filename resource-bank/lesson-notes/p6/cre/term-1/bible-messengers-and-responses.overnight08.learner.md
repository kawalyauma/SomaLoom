### A message and a messenger

A message is information or an instruction passed from one person to another. A messenger is the person who carries it. In Bible stories, Christians believe God called people to deliver messages and help others.

The message and the messenger are not the same. A messenger must try to pass on the message faithfully. People may listen, question, delay or refuse to act.

### Abraham was called to go

In Genesis 12:1–9, God called Abram (later called Abraham) to leave his homeland and go to a land that God would show him. God promised blessing and a future for his family.

Abram responded by setting out with his household. He built altars as he travelled. His story teaches Christians about trust and willing obedience, even when the full journey is not yet known.

### Moses was called to help his people

In Exodus 3:1–13, Moses encountered God at a burning bush. God told him to go to Pharaoh and lead the Israelites out of slavery. Moses was worried about whether he was suitable. God promised to be with him.

Exodus 6:8–9 explains that the Israelites at first did not listen to Moses because they were distressed by harsh slavery. A true message may still be hard for suffering people to hear.

### Jeremiah was called while young

In Jeremiah 1:4–9, Jeremiah was called to speak God’s words. He worried that he was too young and did not know how to speak. God reassured him and gave him words to speak.

This story encourages young people to use their abilities responsibly. It does not mean a child must accept unsafe demands from anyone who claims to speak for God.

### What the stories teach

Abraham acted by travelling. Moses asked questions before accepting a difficult task. Jeremiah expressed fear but received reassurance. The Israelites initially struggled to listen. Different responses appear in the same Bible theme.

At school, a responsible messenger reports information accurately, avoids rumours and asks a trusted adult for help when a message seems unsafe.
