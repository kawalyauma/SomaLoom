### What Christians mean by salvation

Salvation means being rescued from sin and restored to a good relationship with God. Christians believe that Jesus Christ is the promised Saviour or Redeemer. A redeemer is someone who rescues or brings freedom.

The promise of salvation is a Christian belief that God planned to save humanity. The New Testament tells how people prepared for Jesus and responded to him.

### The message brought to Mary

Luke 1:26–38 tells how the angel Gabriel visited Mary in Nazareth. Gabriel told her she would have a son named Jesus. He explained that the child would have a special role in God’s plan.

Mary was troubled and asked how this could happen. After receiving an explanation, she accepted the responsibility. Christians remember her response as an example of faith and willingness.

### John the Baptist prepares people

Luke 3:3–16 describes John the Baptist preaching repentance and baptising people. Repentance means recognising wrongdoing, turning away from it and changing how one lives.

John told people to share with those in need. He told tax collectors not to demand more than required and soldiers not to use threats or false accusations to take money. He pointed people towards someone greater who was coming.

John’s message was not simply about saying sorry. It called for honest actions that show a changed heart.

### Two messages with one hope

Gabriel announced the coming birth of Jesus to Mary. John called the people to prepare by turning away from wrong and living fairly. In Christian teaching, both messages are connected with God’s plan of salvation.

Mary received a personal announcement. John delivered a public message to many people. Their roles were different, but both are important in the Gospel accounts.

### Living out the message

A pupil who cheats can repent by admitting the truth, accepting a fair consequence and choosing honesty next time. A pupil with extra food can share appropriately with a classmate in need.

Christians also practise prayer, forgiveness and care for others. Real change is shown by behaviour, not words alone.
