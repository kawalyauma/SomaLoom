# PRIMARY FIVE ENGLISH GRAMMAR
**Term One | Week Two | Lesson Three**

## THE PRESENT PERFECT PASSIVE
**Topic:** Tenses

### What you will learn
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Explain the meaning of the present perfect passive.
2. Identify the helping verbs used in the tense.
3. Change suitable present perfect sentences from active to passive.
4. Write correct present perfect passive sentences.

### 1. Meaning
The **present perfect passive** tells us that an action has been completed, but we give more attention to the person or thing receiving the action than to the person doing it.

Compare these two sentences:
- **Active:** The pupils have cleaned the classroom.
- **Passive:** The classroom has been cleaned by the pupils.

Both sentences describe the same completed action. In the passive sentence, *the classroom* comes first because it receives the action.

### 2. How to form the present perfect passive

**Subject receiving the action + has/have + been + past participle (+ by + doer).**

| Subject | Helping verbs | Past participle | Rest of sentence |
|---|---|---|---|
| The gate | has been | painted | by the caretaker. |
| The books | have been | arranged | on the shelf. |
| Our classroom | has been | swept | this morning. |
| The mangoes | have been | picked | by the children. |

Use **has been** with one person or thing: *the boy, the desk, she, it*.
Use **have been** with more than one person or thing and with *I, you, we, they*.

**Remember:** A passive sentence needs **been** before the past participle. We say *has been washed*, not *has washed* when the subject receives the action.

### 3. Active and passive sentences
In the active voice, the subject does the action. In the passive voice, the subject receives the action.

| Active | Present perfect passive |
|---|---|
| Amina has washed the plates. | The plates have been washed by Amina. |
| The teacher has marked the books. | The books have been marked by the teacher. |
| The farmers have harvested the maize. | The maize has been harvested by the farmers. |
| The nurse has cleaned the room. | The room has been cleaned by the nurse. |

Notice that **has/have** depends on the new subject, not on the person who did the action.

### 4. Positive, negative and question forms
**Positive:** The food has been cooked.

**Negative:** The food has not been cooked.

**Question:** Has the food been cooked?

**Short answer:** Yes, it has. / No, it has not.

With plural subjects:
- The uniforms have been washed.
- The uniforms have not been washed.
- Have the uniforms been washed?
- Yes, they have. / No, they have not.

### 5. When do we mention the doer?
We can add **by + doer** when it is important to say who did the action.
- The school garden has been watered **by the pupils**.
- The football has been found. (We do not know or need to say who found it.)

### 6. Common mistakes and corrections

| Incorrect | Correct |
|---|---|
| The door has painted. | The door has been painted. |
| The desks has been cleaned. | The desks have been cleaned. |
| The letter have been written. | The letter has been written. |
| The eggs have been ate. | The eggs have been eaten. |
| The room has been clean. | The room has been cleaned. |

### 7. Worked examples
**Example A. Change to passive:** The gardener has cut the grass.

The object *the grass* becomes the new subject. Use *has been* because *grass* is treated as singular. The past participle of *cut* is *cut*.

**Answer:** The grass has been cut by the gardener.

**Example B. Complete:** The oranges ______ ______ ______ (sell).

The oranges are plural, so use *have been*. The past participle of *sell* is *sold*.

**Answer:** The oranges **have been sold**.

**Example C. Make a question:** The chairs have been repaired.

Move *have* before the subject.

**Answer:** **Have the chairs been repaired?**

### Remember
- The passive subject **receives** the action.
- Use **has/have + been + past participle**.
- Use **has been** with a singular subject and **have been** with a plural subject.
- Add **by + doer** only when it is useful.
