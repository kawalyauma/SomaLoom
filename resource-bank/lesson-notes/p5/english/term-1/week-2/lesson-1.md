# Irregular Verbs

**Name:** __________________________________  **Class:** __________  **Date:** __________________

## What you will learn
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. identify irregular verbs;
2. name their **base**, **past-simple** and **past-participle** forms; and
3. use the past simple and past participle correctly in sentences.

## 1. What is a verb?
A **verb** is a word that shows an action or a state. For example: *run, eat, write, go, be*.

Some verbs are **regular**. We usually add **-ed** or **-d** to make their past-simple and past-participle forms.

| Base form | Past simple | Past participle |
|---|---|---|
| play | played | played |
| clean | cleaned | cleaned |
| love | loved | loved |

**Irregular verbs** do not all follow this **-ed** pattern. Their past forms may change in different ways. Some do not change at all.

- *go → went → gone* (all three forms are different)
- *buy → bought → bought* (the last two forms are the same)
- *cut → cut → cut* (all three forms are the same)

## 3. When do we use each form?

### Base form
The **base form** is the dictionary form of the verb. We often use it after *to* and after *can*.

- I want **to write** a letter.
- Amina can **sing** well.

### Past simple
Use the **past-simple** form for an action completed in the past. You may see time words such as *yesterday, last week* or *this morning* (when the morning has finished).

- Yesterday, Musa **went** to the market.
- Our teacher **gave** us an exercise last Friday.
- The pupils **wrote** their names on the books.

Do not add *has*, *have* or *had* before a past-simple form in these examples.

### Past participle
Use the **past participle** after **has**, **have** or **had** in the verb patterns we are practising.

- **Has + past participle:** Sarah **has eaten** her lunch.
- **Have + past participle:** The children **have gone** home.
- **Had + past participle:** By the time the bell rang, I **had finished** my work. (*Finished* is regular.)
- **Had + irregular past participle:** By the time the rain started, Father **had driven** to town.

**Compare carefully:**

| Past simple | Has / have / had + past participle |
|---|---|
| I **saw** the bird yesterday. | I **have seen** that bird before. |
| Joan **broke** the cup. | Joan **has broken** the cup. |
| We **took** the books. | We **have taken** the books. |
| He **did** his homework. | He **has done** his homework. |

## 4. Common mistakes and corrections

| Incorrect | Correct | Why? |
|---|---|---|
| She **goed** to school. | She **went** to school. | The past simple of *go* is *went*. |
| I **have saw** a lion. | I **have seen** a lion. | After *have*, use *seen*. |
| They **has eaten** lunch. | They **have eaten** lunch. | Use *have* with *they*. |
| He **has wrote** a story. | He **has written** a story. | After *has*, use *written*. |
| We **did went** home. | We **went** home. | Do not use *did* before *went* in an affirmative statement. |
| She **did not went** to class. | She **did not go** to class. | After *did not*, use the base form. |

## 5. Worked examples

**Example 1: Complete the sentence.** “Yesterday, the farmer ______ (bring) bananas.”

*Step 1:* The word *yesterday* shows a completed past action.  
*Step 2:* The past simple of *bring* is *brought*.  
**Answer:** Yesterday, the farmer **brought** bananas.

**Example 2: Complete the sentence.** “The girls have ______ (sing) the song.”

*Step 1:* The sentence contains *have*.  
*Step 2:* Use the past participle of *sing*: *sung*.  
**Answer:** The girls have **sung** the song.

**Example 3: Correct the mistake.** “John has ate his posho.”

The form after *has* must be the past participle, *eaten*.  
**Answer:** John has **eaten** his posho.
