Look at the sky on a clear night. You may see the Moon, stars and sometimes planets. They are part of a much larger universe.

## What is in the universe?

The universe includes all space and everything in it: stars, planets, moons, gas, dust and galaxies.

A star is a huge, hot ball of gas that produces its own light. The Sun is our nearest star.

A planet is a large, nearly round body that travels around a star. Planets do not produce their own visible light; we see them because they reflect light.

A natural satellite, or moon, travels around a planet. Earth has one natural Moon.

A galaxy is a very large group of stars, gas and dust held together by gravity. Our Solar System is in the Milky Way galaxy.

## Our Solar System

The Solar System consists of the Sun and the objects held by its gravity, including planets, moons, asteroids and comets.

There are eight planets. From nearest to farthest from the Sun: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune.

Earth is the third planet from the Sun. Venus is closer to the Sun than Earth, while Mars is farther away.

Pluto is classified as a dwarf planet, not one of the eight main planets.

## Rotation and revolution

Rotation is the spinning of Earth on its axis. Earth rotates from west to east. One complete cycle of day and night takes about 24 hours.

The side of Earth facing the Sun receives sunlight and has daytime. The side turned away has night. As Earth rotates, a place moves between day and night.

Revolution is Earth’s journey around the Sun. One revolution takes about 365¼ days (one year).

Do not confuse the two: rotation causes the daily change between day and night; revolution, together with Earth’s tilted axis, is linked to the seasons.

## Worked examples

Example 1 — Classify these objects: Sun = star; Earth = planet; Moon = natural satellite.

Example 2 — A place turns away from the Sun as Earth rotates. What happens there? It moves into night.

Example 3 — Which planets are beside Earth in order from the Sun? Venus comes before Earth; Mars comes after Earth.

## Safe sky observation

Observe the Moon and stars from a safe place with an adult. Never look directly at the Sun, even through ordinary sunglasses.
