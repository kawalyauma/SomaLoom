The Sun does not reach its highest point at the same moment everywhere on Earth. We use longitude to understand this difference. We also use agreed clock times so that people can plan together.

## Important meanings

Longitude is distance east or west of the Prime Meridian (0°), measured in degrees. Lines of longitude join the North Pole to the South Pole.

Local solar time is time based on the position of the Sun at a particular place. Solar noon occurs when the Sun reaches its highest point in the sky.

Standard time is the agreed clock time used in a country or region. A time zone is an area that uses the same standard time.

Uganda uses East Africa Time (EAT), which is 3 hours ahead of Coordinated Universal Time (UTC). Uganda uses EAT throughout the year.

## Why local solar times differ

Earth rotates from west to east. Places farther east reach solar noon earlier, so their local solar time is ahead of places farther west.

Earth turns about 360° in 24 hours. Therefore 15° of longitude corresponds to about 1 hour of solar-time difference, and 1° corresponds to about 4 minutes.

This 15° rule is a useful geography calculation. Actual national time-zone boundaries do not always follow lines exactly 15° apart.

## Worked examples

Example 1 — At 0° longitude the solar time is 10:00. What is the idealised solar time at 30°E? Difference = 30 ÷ 15 = 2 hours. East is ahead, so 10:00 + 2 hours = 12:00.

Example 2 — At 0° longitude the solar time is 09:00. What is the idealised solar time at 15°W? Difference = 15 ÷ 15 = 1 hour. West is behind, so 09:00 − 1 hour = 08:00.

Example 3 — When it is 08:00 UTC, what is the official time in Uganda? Uganda is UTC+3, so 08:00 + 3 hours = 11:00 EAT.

## Remember the difference

Local solar time depends on the Sun and longitude. Official standard time depends on the time zone chosen for a place.

Do not treat the idealised 15° calculation as the legal clock time for every country.
