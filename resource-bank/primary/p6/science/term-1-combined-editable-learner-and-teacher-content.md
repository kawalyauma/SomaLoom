## Unit 1: Classification: vertebrates and invertebrates

### Learner notes

Animals can be grouped by shared features. Vertebrates have a backbone: goats, tilapia, hens, frogs and snakes. Invertebrates have no backbone: snails, insects and earthworms. Mammals feed their young on milk. Birds have feathers. Fish generally have gills. Reptiles have dry scaly skin. Many amphibians start as aquatic young and change form as they grow.

## Unit 2: Mammals and survival

### Learner notes

Mammals have a backbone and the mothers produce milk. Most have hair or fur; a few, such as platypuses, lay eggs. Goats, cows, humans and bats are mammals. Broad molars grind plant food. Lungs breathe air. Protect animals by providing appropriate food, clean water and shelter.

## Unit 3: Fish and amphibians

### Learner notes

Fish, including tilapia and Nile perch, are vertebrates that breathe mainly through gills and use fins for swimming. Amphibians include frogs and toads. A tadpole often breathes through gills before undergoing metamorphosis. Most adult frogs use lungs and moist skin for gas exchange. Protect water sources from pollution.

## Unit 4: Invertebrate groups

### Learner notes

Arthropods have jointed legs and an outer skeleton. Adult insects have six legs; spiders have eight. Crabs belong to crustaceans. Snails are molluscs with soft bodies. Earthworms have segmented bodies and help improve soil structure. Bees pollinate flowers; some mosquitoes spread disease.

## Unit 5: Caring for animals

### Learner notes

Animals contribute to food webs, seed dispersal and pollination. Domestic cattle provide milk, meat and manure. Care for livestock with water, healthy feed, shelter, hygiene and veterinary assistance. Observe wild animals from a safe distance and protect nesting places, rivers and wetlands.

## Unit 6: Sound sources and vibrations

### Learner notes

Sound starts when an object vibrates. A plucked rubber band vibrates; a drum membrane vibrates when struck. Thunder and birds are natural sound sources. Bells, radios and whistles are human-made. Sound passes through solids, liquids and gases, but not through a vacuum. Observe a gently plucked band without making loud noises.

## Unit 7: Pitch, loudness and hearing safety

### Learner notes

Pitch tells whether a sound is high or low; it depends on vibration frequency. Loudness relates to vibration amplitude. An echo is a reflected sound heard again. Turn down earphone volume, avoid loud speakers and never push sharp objects into ears.

## Unit 8: The ear and recorded sound

### Learner notes

The pinna collects sound, which travels through the ear canal and vibrates the eardrum. Middle-ear bones transmit vibration to the cochlea. The auditory nerve carries signals to the brain. A microphone converts sound into an electrical signal; a loudspeaker turns an electrical signal into vibrations. Seek medical help for ear pain or discharge.

## Unit 9: Blood circulation and the heart

### Learner notes

The heart pumps blood through two main circuits. The right side sends blood to the lungs to obtain oxygen; the left side sends oxygen-rich blood to the body. The human heart has a right and left atrium and a right and left ventricle. Valves prevent backflow. Diagram: Body → right heart → lungs → left heart → body.

## Unit 10: Blood vessels and blood components

### Learner notes

Arteries carry blood away from the heart and veins towards it. Capillaries allow exchange with tissues. Plasma is the liquid part of blood. Red cells carry oxygen; white cells help fight infection; platelets help clotting. HIV can be transmitted through infected blood entering the bloodstream, not through handshakes or mosquito bites. Never share needles or razors.

## Unit 11: Alcohol, tobacco and medicines

### Learner notes

Alcohol can impair judgement and coordination. Tobacco smoking harms the heart and lungs, and second-hand smoke also causes harm. Medicines must be used as directed by a trained health worker and stored in labelled containers away from children. Never taste unknown tablets or accept drugs offered by peers.
